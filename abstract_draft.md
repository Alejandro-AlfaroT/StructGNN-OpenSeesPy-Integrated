# Abstract — LSTM/GNN Hybrid Surrogate Model for RC SMRF Seismic Response

Nonlinear time history analysis (NTHA) is the most reliable method for evaluating the seismic response of reinforced concrete special moment resisting frames (RC SMRFs), but its computational cost makes large-scale applications such as fragility analysis and regional risk assessment impractical. This work investigates the hypothesis that a hybrid deep learning surrogate—combining a graph neural network (GNN) to capture structural topology with a long short-term memory (LSTM) network to capture temporal dynamics—can predict nonlinear seismic response at a small fraction of the cost of direct simulation. While GNNs have previously shown strong accuracy and generalization for static structural analysis, their extension to nonlinear dynamic seismic response remains largely unexplored.

An eight-story, three-bay by three-bay RC SMRF was designed and modeled in OpenSees using concentrated-plasticity elements with Ibarra–Medina–Krawinkler hinges and rigid floor diaphragms. An automated pipeline performs gravity, modal, pushover, and bidirectional NTHA, using ground motions from the PEER NGA-West2 database, and exports each simulation as a graph dataset of node, element, and response time history features for surrogate training. In the hybrid architecture, GNN message passing propagates information across the structural graph while the LSTM models the evolution of response over time.

Preliminary results validate the full simulation-to-dataset pipeline. NTHA under the 1952 Kern County record pair (PGA 0.16–0.18 g) completed all 5,435 steps with stable convergence, producing peak story drifts near 1.4% and a fundamental elastic period of 0.73 s consistent with the design. Pushover and mechanism checks confirmed the expected strong-column/weak-beam yielding behavior, and the exported graph datasets capture the geometry, member properties, and response histories needed for training.

These results demonstrate a working end-to-end simulation-to-dataset framework, supporting the feasibility of the proposed hybrid surrogate. Ongoing work extends the pipeline to a large ground motion suite and trains the coupled LSTM/GNN model to predict full nonlinear response histories for seismic performance assessment.
